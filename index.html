About Me

I am a PhD student at Example University, working on reliable and adaptive machine learning. My research explores how learning systems can acquire useful knowledge, evaluate their own behavior, and improve through interaction. I am particularly interested in connecting careful empirical evaluation with methods that remain useful as tasks and environments change. I previously received my bachelor's degree from Example Institute.

Open to collaboration. I enjoy working with researchers across machine learning, language models, and interactive systems. Feel free to reach out if you would like to discuss research ideas, open-source projects, or potential collaborations.

Research Overview

“Building learning systems that improve through experience while remaining reliable, understandable, and easy to evaluate.”

Reliable Learning & Evaluation

Developing evaluation protocols and learning objectives that help distinguish meaningful progress from improvements tied to a particular benchmark, data split, or experimental setting.

Continual Adaptation & Memory

Studying how models acquire, retain, and revise knowledge over time, with an emphasis on practical memory mechanisms, transfer across tasks, and transparent experimental comparisons.
